# Jetcost — Business Context

> Condensed context for agents. For the full reference see [core/business/overview.md](/core/business/overview.md).

## What Jetcost Is

Jetcost is a flight metasearch engine. It aggregates flight offers from hundreds of partners — OTAs, airlines, booking platforms — and presents them to users searching for flights. Jetcost does not sell flights directly: its role is to connect users with the right offer and redirect them to the partner's platform to complete the booking.

Revenue is generated when users click out to partners (Core revenue, CPC or CPA model) and through sponsored placements and display advertising (Media revenue).

## The Two Products

Jetcost operates two distinct products:

**Web** — the primary product, accessed via browser on both desktop and mobile. This is where the majority of traffic and revenue originates. The web experience is responsive and shared across devices.

**App** — the mobile application (iOS and Android). The app has different monetisation characteristics: it is strategically important for CPA markets and is the target of web-to-app (W2A) initiatives, which aim to shift users from web to app to improve conversion and revenue quality.

The two products are independent: features, configurations, and experiments are not necessarily aligned between web and app. A feature active on web may not exist on app, and vice versa. Always check the scope of a feature before drawing conclusions about platform-wide behaviour.

## The Funnel

A user session follows a sequential funnel:

1. **Session** — user lands on Jetcost
2. **Search** — user performs a flight search
3. **Impression** — search results are displayed
4. **Click** — user selects an offer and is redirected to the partner
5. **Booking** — user completes the purchase on the partner platform (relevant for CPA deals)

Key conversion rates: STR (session-to-search), CTR (click-through rate), CR (conversion rate from click to booking).

## Geographic Presence

Jetcost operates across multiple countries and languages. Markets differ in deal structure, user behaviour, and revenue potential. The web product covers all markets; the app has a more selective geographic footprint with stronger presence in high-CPA markets.
